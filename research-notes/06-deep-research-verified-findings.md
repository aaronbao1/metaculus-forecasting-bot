# Deep-Research Workflow: Verified Findings (adversarially checked claims)

*Output of a 104-agent search → fetch → 3-vote adversarial verification → synthesis pipeline, June 2026. Stats: 22 sources fetched, 109 claims extracted, 25 verified (25 confirmed, 0 killed), merged to 11 findings. Each claim survived 3-vote adversarial refutation attempts.*

## Executive summary

As of mid-2026, the evidence-backed recipe for a state-of-the-art Metaculus forecasting bot is: (1) use the strongest available reasoning model, since base model choice dominates scaffolding complexity (o3-class models beat elaborate multi-agent pipelines built on weaker models); (2) ensemble multiple forecasts across several models and runs, aggregating with median or trimmed mean — the single most consistently validated technique, associated with ~+1,800 tournament points and with LLM-crowd accuracy statistically matching a 925-person human crowd; (3) build a structured research pipeline (AskNews, Perplexity, Serper + scraping) that produces explicit outside-view (base rate) and inside-view reports, because naive news dumping into prompts can actually hurt accuracy; (4) correct for the LLMs' systematic positive/acquiescence bias (e.g., mapping ~30% forecasts down to ~20%) and push toward more decisive forecasts, since bots lose to human Pros on discrimination, not calibration; and (5) anchor on community/crowd forecasts when available, which improved GPT-4/Claude accuracy 17-28% and characterized every top ForecastBench configuration. Humans still led through Q2 2025 — Metaculus Pros beat the top bots every quarter and superforecasters beat the best LLM on ForecastBench (0.081-0.096 vs 0.101-0.122 Brier) — so the frontier is closing the discrimination gap, with outcome-based RLVR fine-tuning emerging as a promising but tournament-unproven lever. Practically, build on the official metac-bot-template / forecasting-tools stack with GitHub Actions automation, and test against custom-created resolution questions, the practice most strongly correlated with tournament success (+2,216 points).

## Verified findings

### 1. Human expert forecasters still outperformed the best LLM bots throughout 2024 to mid-2025, on both ForecastBench and the Metaculus AI Benchmark tournaments, but the gap is real, quantified, and (non-monotonically) narrowing. On ForecastBench, superforecasters (Brier 0.096 on the human-comparison subset; 0.081 difficulty-adjusted in the Oct 2025 update) significantly beat the top LLM (p < 0.001), with the best LLM configuration being Claude 3.5 Sonnet with scratchpad prompting plus prediction-market freeze values at Brier 0.122 — roughly tied with the public median (0.121). On Metaculus, the head-to-head Pro-vs-top-bot scores were: Q3 2024: -11.3 (p = 0.036, significant, 55 bots); Q4 2024: -8.9 (95% CI [-18.8, 1], p = 0.079, not significant, 44 bots) with the Pro median beating all 32 active bots (29/32 significantly); Q2 2025: -20.03 (95% CI [-28.63, -11.41], p = 0.00001) with every individual Pro beating every individual bot. LLMs already beat the median public forecaster by 2025; FRI projects LLM-superforecaster parity around late 2026 (95% CI Dec 2025 - Jan 2028).

- **Confidence:** high (verification vote: 3-0 on all six merged claims)
- **Evidence:** ForecastBench (ICLR 2025): 'expert forecasters outperform the top-performing LLM (p-value < 0.001)'; Table 2 top LLM = Claude-3.5-Sonnet / freeze values / scratchpad, Brier 0.122 vs superforecasters 0.096. ### 2. Base model choice matters more than scaffolding or prompt complexity. In Q3 2024, Metaculus's simple single-shot GPT-4o template bot (mf-bot-1) finished near the top of 55 bots — beating many multi-agent, prompt-chained architectures — while the identical prompt on GPT-3.5 finished dead last, worse than always forecasting 50%. In Q2 2025, Metaculus's standardized template-bot comparison found o3 the best forecasting model (metac-o3 + AskNews placed 2nd overall in the entire tournament), and Metaculus explicitly concluded 'the most important factor for accurate forecasting is the base model used, with additional prompting providing marginal gains', with o4-mini-high and DeepSeek-R1 the best performance per dollar.

- **Confidence:** high (verification vote: 3-0 on both merged claims)
- **Evidence:** Q3 2024: 'a single shot prompt like mf-bot-1 finished near the top compared to the many bots that used multiple agents and prompt chaining techniques... The Metaculus bot powered by GPT-3.5 finished last out of 55 bots, worse than simply forecasting 50% on every question' (official rank 4/55 weighted for mf-bot-1; controlled same-prompt comparison). Q2 2025: 'OpenAI's o3 significantly outperformed even the next best non-o3 model'; 'The most cost-effective models would be o4-mini-high and DeepSeek-R1'. Merged claims [11], [22].
- **Sources:** https://www.metaculus.com/notebooks/28784/aibq3results/ · https://forum.effectivealtruism.org/posts/F2stjK9wHSy3HPEC9/q2-ai-benchmark-results-pros-maintain-clear-lead

### 3. Ensembling/aggregation is the most consistently validated technique across both academic research and tournament practice. A 12-LLM ensemble's aggregated predictions on 31 binary questions were statistically indistinguishable from a 925-person human forecasting crowd (equivalent within medium-effect-size bounds), even though individual frontier LLMs underperform human crowd aggregates — replicating the wisdom-of-the-crowd effect for machines. In tournaments, 76% of Q4 2024 prize-winning bots repeated LLM calls and took a median/mean, and in the Q2 2025 bot-maker survey, aggregating multiple forecasts was associated with +1,799 coverage-adjusted points (95% CI +1,017 to +2,582). Both quarterly winners used ensembles (pgodzinai: trimmed mean of 8 calls across 2 models; Panshul42: 5 predictions across 3 models).

- **Confidence:** high (verification vote: 3-0 on all merged claims)
- **Evidence:** Schoenegger et al. (Science Advances, Tetlock co-author, preregistered): 'the LLM crowd outperforms a simple no-information benchmark and is not statistically different from the human crowd' and the two 'are equivalent with respect to medium-effect-size equivalence bounds'. Q4 2024 survey: '76% of winners repeated calls to an LLM and took a median/mean'. Q2 2025 survey: 'median/mean/aggregate of multiple forecasts... averaged +1,799 points higher (95% CI: +1,017 to +2,582)'. Merged claims [3], [4], [12-part], [15-part].
- **Sources:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.19379 · https://www.lesswrong.com/posts/P8YwCvHoF2FHQoHjF/metaculus-q4-ai-benchmarking-bots-are-closing-the-gap · https://forum.effectivealtruism.org/posts/F2stjK9wHSy3HPEC9/q2-ai-benchmark-results-pros-maintain-clear-lead

### 4. Anchoring on crowd/community forecasts measurably improves LLM forecasting accuracy. Exposing GPT-4 and Claude 2 to the median human crowd prediction improved accuracy by 17-28% (with simple averaging of human and machine forecasts working even better than prompt exposure). On ForecastBench, all top-performing LLM configurations had access to prediction-market freeze values (crowd forecasts), and models without them were less accurate. Practical implication: a Metaculus bot should incorporate community predictions and market prices where the tournament rules permit (note: AI Benchmark questions typically hide the community prediction, so this applies to external market signals and to tournaments where CP is visible).

- **Confidence:** high (verification vote: 3-0 on both merged claims)
- **Evidence:** Schoenegger et al.: 'both models' forecasting accuracy benefits from exposure to the median human prediction as information, improving accuracy by between 17% and 28%'. ForecastBench: 'The top-performing LLMs all had access to the crowd forecast on market questions (the freeze values...). The top-performing model without access to the crowd forecast on market questions was less accurate than models with access.' Merged claims [1-part], [6].
- **Sources:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.19379 · https://arxiv.org/abs/2409.19839

### 5. LLM forecasters exhibit a systematic positive/acquiescence bias — predicting 'yes' too often — that a competitive bot must explicitly correct. In the silicon-crowd study, mean model predictions were significantly above 50% (M = 57.35, t = 86.20, p < 0.001) despite a near-even split of resolutions. In Q3 2024 Metaculus data, when the bot team forecast 29%, events occurred only 12% of the time; Metaculus noted this 'can be addressed fairly easily with an appropriate adjustment, so that when the bots say 30% the adjustment lowers their forecast to 20%'. Follow-up work in 2025-2026 replicates the yes-bias in newer models. The Q4 2024 winner explicitly prompted against this bias.

- **Confidence:** high (verification vote: 3-0 on both merged claims)
- **Evidence:** Schoenegger et al.: 'We also observe an acquiescence effect, with mean model predictions being significantly above 50%, despite an almost even split of positive and negative resolutions.' Metaculus Q3 2024: 'they have a positive bias: they tend to predict things will happen more than they do. When the bots forecast 29%, it happens 12% of the time.' Merged claims [5], [21].
- **Sources:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.19379 · https://www.metaculus.com/notebooks/28784/aibq3results/

### 6. Bots lose to human Pros on discrimination, not calibration — so the highest-leverage improvement is decisiveness, not further calibration tuning. In Q4 2024, both the Pros and the top bot (pgodzinai) were well-calibrated, but the Pros' average forecast on Yes-resolving questions was 44 percentage points higher than on No-resolving questions versus only 26 points for the top bot, and Pros made more extreme forecasts (bottom quintile 2% vs 7%; top quintile 87% vs 76%). A bot that can gather decisive evidence and commit to extreme probabilities when warranted — without breaking calibration — captures the main remaining human edge.

- **Confidence:** high (verification vote: 3-0)
- **Evidence:** 'The Pros' superior accuracy is not a result of better calibration; it is a result of better discrimination'; 'the discrimination for the bot team is 26 percentage points, far less than the 44 percentage points for the Pros'; 'the bottom quintile of forecasts averaged 2% for the Pros, but was 7% for the top bot. And the top quintile of forecasts was 87% for the Pros, but only 76% for bots.' Single primary source (the tournament operator's own statistical analysis, corroborated across its EA Forum crosspost and Metaculus notebook), verified 3-0. Claim [17].
- **Sources:** https://www.lesswrong.com/posts/P8YwCvHoF2FHQoHjF/metaculus-q4-ai-benchmarking-bots-are-closing-the-gap

### 7. Retrieval design matters more than retrieval presence: naively adding recent news to prompts did not help and sometimes hurt (ForecastBench: Claude 3.5 Sonnet's Brier degraded 0.122 -> 0.127 with news added), while Halawi et al.'s carefully engineered retrieval-augmented system (GPT-4 + self-supervised fine-tuned model, query generation, article relevance ranking, summarization, trimmed-mean aggregation) reached Brier 0.179 vs the human crowd's 0.149 (71.5% vs 77.0% accuracy) on 914 held-out post-cutoff binary questions from five platforms — and actually beat the crowd in the selective setting where the crowd was uncertain (crowd prediction 0.3-0.7: system 0.238 vs crowd 0.240; with three selective conditions jointly met, beat the crowd by >1.5 standard errors). Implication for bots: invest in structured multi-step research pipelines with relevance filtering, not raw news dumps, and recognize bots add the most value on genuinely uncertain questions.

- **Confidence:** high (verification vote: 3-0 on all three merged claims)
- **Evidence:** ForecastBench: 'Access to recent topical news related to the questions did not improve performance' (Table 2: 0.122 vs 0.127). Halawi et al. (NeurIPS 2024): 'our averaged Brier score is .179, while the crowd achieves .149... Our accuracy on the test set is 71.5%, whereas the community scores 77.0%'; selective setting: 'our system attains a Brier score of .238 (crowd aggregate: .240)... When all three conditions are jointly met, our system beats the crowd significantly (by more than 1.5 standard errors)'. Note 1.5 SE is below the conventional 1.96 threshold and the paper's SE computation likely underestimates true error. Merged claims [2], [23], [24].
- **Sources:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2409.19839 · https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.18563

### 8. Concrete winning-bot blueprints exist and are open source. Q4 2024 winner pgodzinai (Phil Godzin, built in 15-40 hours): grouped related questions and predicted them in batches for internal consistency; used Perplexity (llama-3.1-sonar-huge-128k-online) and AskNews for research; ran 3 GPT-4o + 5 Claude-3.5-Sonnet-20241022 forecasts; dropped the two most extreme values and averaged the remaining six (trimmed mean). Q2 2025 winner Panshul42: a 6-7 step agentic pipeline using Serper for search and BrightData for HTML scraping; wrote separate outside-view (historical base rates) and inside-view research reports; ensembled five final predictions from Claude Sonnet 3.7/4 (x2), o4-mini (x2), and o3 (x1); open-sourced at github.com/Panshul42/Forecasting_Bot_Q2. Shared design DNA: multi-source research, explicit outside/inside view separation, cross-model ensembling with robust aggregation.

- **Confidence:** high (verification vote: 3-0 on both merged claims)

### 9. Bot-maker surveys reveal that winning is cheap and simple in compute but disciplined in process: Q4 2024 winners used on average only 1.8 different LLMs, ~75% used fewer than 100 LLM calls (50% under 20), and only one winning bot used a fine-tuned LLM. The practice most strongly associated with Q2 2025 success was testing bots on custom-created resolution questions (+2,216 coverage-adjusted points, 95% CI +912 to +3,519), even more than forecast aggregation (+1,799). Building an evaluation harness with your own resolvable questions is a top-priority investment.

- **Confidence:** high (verification vote: 3-0 on both merged claims)
- **Evidence:** Q4 2024 survey (13 respondents): 'Only one bot used a fine tuned LLM'; 'Winners on average used 1.8 different LLMs'; 'Around 75% of winners used less than 100 LLM calls, 50% of participants used less than 20'. Q2 2025 survey (19 respondents, 7 top-10): custom question testing '+2,216 points on average... (95% confidence interval = +912 to +3,519)'. Both surveys are correlational, small-n, self-reported — the sources themselves warn 'correlation does not necessarily mean causation'. Merged claims [12], [15].
- **Sources:** https://www.lesswrong.com/posts/P8YwCvHoF2FHQoHjF/metaculus-q4-ai-benchmarking-bots-are-closing-the-gap · https://forum.effectivealtruism.org/posts/F2stjK9wHSy3HPEC9/q2-ai-benchmark-results-pros-maintain-clear-lead

### 10. Outcome-based RL fine-tuning is an emerging frontier: a compact 14B model (DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Qwen-14B) trained with RLVR (reward = negative Brier) on ~10k Polymarket questions plus retrieved news headlines matched or slightly exceeded o1's accuracy (ensemble soft-Brier 0.190 vs 0.202, p < 0.05) while being substantially better calibrated (ECE 0.062 vs 0.093). Critically for anyone attempting this: standard GRPO's per-question standard-deviation normalization flattens the reward signal and causes severe overconfidence (39.3% of predictions piled into the 0-10%/90-100% buckets, many exactly 0 or 1); removing the normalization (Modified GRPO) or using ReMax's baseline-subtracted advantages preserves large forecast-error signals and restores calibration. Independent work (arXiv 2508.11800, Dr. GRPO) corroborates the GRPO overconfidence mechanism. Note Polymarket itself (Brier 0.151) still beat all models, and tournament evidence shows fine-tuning remains rare among winners.

- **Confidence:** medium (verification vote: 3-0 on both merged claims)
- **Evidence:** TMLR (peer-reviewed, Nov 2025): 'a compact (14B) reasoning model can be trained to match or surpass the predictive accuracy of frontier models like o1, while greatly improving probabilistic calibration'; 'By removing per-question normalisation (Modified GRPO) or using ReMax's baseline-based approach, we better preserve the impact of large deviations'. Confidence medium despite 3-0 votes because both claims rest on a single research group's paper (Lightning Rod Labs, mild COI), evaluation is binary Polymarket questions on one hold-out split, the accuracy edge over o1 is marginal and ensemble-dependent, and the approach has not yet been validated in Metaculus tournaments. Merged claims [7], [8].
- **Sources:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.17989

- **Confidence:** high (verification vote: 3-0 on both merged claims)
- **Evidence:** README: 'main.py: Our recommended template option that uses the forecasting-tools package to handle a lot of stuff in the background for you (such as API calls)... main_with_no_framework.py: A copy of main.py but implemented with minimal dependencies'; 'the bot will keep forecasting on new questions automatically every 20 minutes'. Verified against live repo code (workflow YAML cron schedules and main.py tournament-mode flags), current as of June 2026 (references SummerTemplateBot2026). Merged claims [18], [19].

## Caveats (workflow self-assessment)

Time-sensitivity is the dominant caveat: the most recent verified tournament data is Q2 2025 (published Oct 2025), and model-specific findings (o3 best, o4-mini/DeepSeek-R1 most cost-effective, Claude 3.5 Sonnet top on ForecastBench) will be stale relative to the mid-2026 model landscape; the human-bot gap was narrowing (~0.016 Brier points/year per FRI) with parity projected for late 2026, but the Q2 2025 result (-20.03) shows the trend is non-monotonic, possibly due to harder question mixes. Several findings rest on small samples: the silicon-crowd equivalence used only 31 binary questions with 2023-era models (low statistical power); bot-maker surveys had 13-19 self-selected respondents with wide CIs and explicitly correlational framing; the 30%->20% bias adjustment was derived in-sample from one quarter. The RLVR fine-tuning finding comes from a single research group with mild conflict of interest, mitigated by TMLR peer review and public data release. Nearly all verified evidence concerns binary questions — the verified claim set contains essentially nothing on numeric/continuous CDF construction or multiple-choice handling. Finally, several sections of the original research question produced no claims that survived verification: Metaculus scoring mechanics and strategy (spot peer score, extremize-vs-hedge tradeoffs), classic Tetlock/Good Judgment Project forecasting science, FutureSearch's work, and Q3 2025-Q2 2026 tournament results — the final document should treat those areas as unverified or fill them from primary sources directly rather than from this claim set.

## Open questions flagged by the workflow

- How do Metaculus tournament scoring rules (spot peer score being zero-sum among bots, baseline scoring) change optimal bot strategy — specifically when extremizing beats hedging toward the median bot forecast — given no claims on scoring mechanics survived verification?
- What are the best-validated techniques for numeric/continuous questions (CDF construction, distribution width, tail handling) and multiple-choice questions, where essentially all verified evidence covered only binary questions?
- Have bots closed or reversed the gap with Pro forecasters in the Q3 2025 through Q2 2026 tournaments, and did FRI's late-2026 LLM-superforecaster parity projection hold as frontier models advanced?
- Is the bots' discrimination deficit (26pp vs 44pp) caused by retrieval limitations (failure to find decisive evidence) or by model-level conservatism — and can post-hoc extremization capture the missing edge without degrading the calibration bots already have?
